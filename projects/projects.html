The Story:

For physics-informed deep learning, I used the DeepXDE library with the JAX backend.
I originally completed a proof-of-concept using PyTorch as the backend, but it was slow. I switched to JAX so that DeepXDE can take full advantage of CUDA and JAX's superior JIT compilation.
This came at a cost, however, as JAX was the least supported DeepXDE backend.
After training with Adam, I would finish off the model with L-BFGS. Here's the catch: DeepXDE w/ JAX doesn't support L-BFGS optimization.

Instead of admitting defeat and falling back to PyTorch, I wouldn't take no for an answer.
I was going to manually implement a GPU-efficient training sequence for L-BFGS.
This is the moment that transformed the project from a glorified homework assignment to advanced implementation.

I could no longer rely on DeepXDE's abstract "black box" methods and had to dip my toes in low-level ML engineering.
As someone who's never worked with machine learning before, I had to figure out how to manually write optimization loops. It was an absolute nightmare but I learned an incredible amount from it.
I extracted the raw tensors out of my DeepXDE model, processed it through my own JAX/Optax pipeline, and injected it back into DeepXDE.
In the process, I had to figure out roughly how Optax solves and how to deal with L-BFGS being second-order, learn performance optimization with JIT compilation, and understand host-device synchronization.

Main takeaway:

Coming in knowing next to nothing about neural networks, in less than a week I used my extensive math background and self-taught coding skills to learn:
  • Fundamentals of Neural Networks
    • Layer structure, activation functions, training/optimization processes, etc.
    • The calculus behind backpropogation
  • PINNs
    • PINN Training (collocation points & automatic differentiation, PDE residual loss, etc.)
    • Using DeepXDE to approximate PDEs
  • Future Applied Math Topics and Modeling
    • Traffic flow modeling using the LWR model (dealing with shocks, numerical stability, etc.)
    • PDE solution methods
    • Advanced data visualization with Matplotlib
  • Low-Level ML Engineering
    • JAX and Optax
      • Inner workings of how computers handle training/optimizing
      • GPU computing with JIT compilation
      • ML Library interoperability
